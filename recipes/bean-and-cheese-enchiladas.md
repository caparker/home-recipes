## Ingredients

### Enchilada sauce
- 3 tbsp vegetable oil
- 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 3 tbsp chili powder (mild, like ancho-based)
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1/2 tsp garlic powder
- 1/2 tsp dried oregano
- 1/4 tsp ground cinnamon
- 2 cups vegetable broth
- 1 (8 oz) can tomato sauce
- 3/4 tsp kosher salt

### Bean & cheese filling
- 2 (15 oz) cans pinto beans, drained (reserve 1/4 cup liquid)
- 1 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1/2 medium yellow onion, finely diced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 2 cups shredded Monterey Jack or Cheddar (divided: 1.5 cups filling, 0.5 cup topping)

### Roasted corn topping
- 2 cups frozen corn kernels (thawed) or fresh
- 1 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1/4 tsp kosher salt
- 1/4 tsp chili powder
- 2 tbsp chopped cilantro
- 1 tbsp lime juice
- 2 tbsp crumbled cotija or feta (optional)

### To assemble
- 12 corn tortillas (6-inch)
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil (for warming tortillas)

## Equipment

- Medium saucepan (sauce)
- Large skillet (filling + corn)
- Blender or immersion blender
- 9x13 baking dish
- Tongs
- Small skillet or comal (warming tortillas)

## Prep-ahead notes

- **Sauce** keeps 5 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen.
- **Bean filling** keeps 3 days refrigerated.
- **Assembled, unbaked enchiladas** can be refrigerated 24 hours; add 10 min to bake time.
- **Freezer-friendly:** assemble in a foil pan, wrap tightly, freeze up to 2 months. Thaw overnight before baking.
- Roasted corn topping is best fresh but can be made 1 day ahead.

## Instructions

### 1. Make the sauce
1. Heat oil in a saucepan over medium. Whisk in flour and cook 1 minute (light roux — don't brown).
2. Whisk in chili powder, cumin, garlic powder, oregano, and cinnamon. Cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
3. Slowly whisk in broth, then tomato sauce and salt. Simmer 5–7 minutes until slightly thickened (should coat a spoon).
4. Blend until smooth (or use immersion blender). Taste and adjust salt.

### 2. Make the filling
1. Heat oil in a large skillet over medium. Cook onion 4 minutes until soft. Add garlic, cumin, and salt; cook 30 seconds.
2. Add pinto beans and reserved bean liquid. Mash with a potato masher or fork to a chunky refried texture (leave some beans whole).
3. Cook 2–3 minutes until thick and scoopable. Remove from heat, stir in 1.5 cups cheese.

### 3. Roast the corn
1. Heat oil in a skillet over medium-high until shimmering. Add corn in a single layer.
2. Cook undisturbed 3–4 minutes until charred spots form. Stir and cook 2 more minutes.
3. Off heat, toss with salt, chili powder, cilantro, and lime juice. Set aside.

### 4. Warm the tortillas
1. Heat a dry skillet or comal over medium. Brush each tortilla lightly with oil.
2. Warm 15–20 seconds per side until pliable. Stack under a towel to keep soft.
   *(Skipping this = cracked tortillas. Non-negotiable step.)*

### 5. Assemble
1. Preheat oven to 375°F.
2. Spread 1/2 cup sauce on the bottom of a 9x13 dish.
3. For each tortilla: dip briefly in sauce (both sides), lay on a plate, spoon ~1/4 cup filling down the center, roll snugly, and place seam-down in the dish.
4. Pour remaining sauce evenly over the top. Sprinkle with remaining 1/2 cup cheese.

### 6. Bake
1. Cover with foil and bake 15 minutes.
2. Uncover and bake 10 more minutes until cheese is bubbly and edges brown.
3. Let rest 5 minutes before serving.

### 7. Top and serve
1. Scatter roasted corn topping across the enchiladas (or serve on the side).
2. Sprinkle cotija/feta if using. Serve with lime wedges.

## Notes

- **Toddler tip:** pull 2 tortillas' worth of plain bean/cheese filling before mixing with sauce; serve as a simple quesadilla or on the side.
- **Spice:** the sauce is mild. Add 1/4 tsp chipotle powder or a chopped chipotle in adobo for heat.
- **Bean swap:** substitute 1 can black beans for 1 can pinto for color contrast.
- **Sauce shortcut:** 2 cups store-bought red enchilada sauce works if pressed for time (Hatch or Frontera are good).
- **Leftovers** reheat well covered at 350°F for 15 min.

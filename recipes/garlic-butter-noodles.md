## Ingredients

- 12 oz whole grain pasta (spaghetti, linguine, or penne)
- 1 large head broccoli (about 1 lb), cut into small florets
- 1 (15 oz) can white beans (cannellini or great northern), drained and rinsed
- 4 tbsp butter
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- 6 cloves garlic, thinly sliced or grated
- 1/2 tsp red pepper flakes (optional)
- 1/2 cup reserved pasta water
- Zest and juice of 1 lemon
- 1/2 cup grated Parmesan, plus more for serving
- 3/4 tsp kosher salt (plus more for pasta water)
- Black pepper
- 2 tbsp chopped fresh parsley (optional)

## Equipment

- Large pot
- Colander
- Large skillet
- Microplane or zester

## Prep-ahead notes

Best eaten fresh — noodles get gummy on reheat. If prepping ahead: chop broccoli and slice garlic earlier in the day. Leftovers keep 2 days; revive with a splash of water and butter.

## Instructions

1. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil. Add pasta and cook according to package directions. In the last 3 minutes, add broccoli florets to the same pot.
2. Reserve 1 cup pasta water, then drain pasta and broccoli together.
3. While pasta cooks, melt butter with olive oil in a large skillet over medium-low heat. Add garlic and red pepper flakes; cook 2–3 minutes until fragrant and just golden. Don't brown.
4. Add white beans and 1/2 cup reserved pasta water. Simmer 2 minutes, gently smashing about a third of the beans with a spoon to thicken the sauce.
5. Add drained pasta and broccoli to the skillet. Toss to coat, adding more pasta water as needed for a silky sauce.
6. Off heat, stir in lemon zest, lemon juice, Parmesan, salt, and plenty of black pepper.
7. Top with parsley and extra Parmesan. Serve immediately.

## Notes

- Toddler tip: pull out plain buttered noodles + broccoli + a few beans before adding lemon/pepper/garlic-heavy sauce.
- Whole grain pasta needs slightly more sauce than white pasta — don't skimp on the pasta water.
- Sub kale (stemmed, chopped) for broccoli in colder months; add with the garlic.
- Add a handful of baby spinach at the end for extra greens.
- Leftover rotisserie-style crispy chickpeas make a great crunchy topping.

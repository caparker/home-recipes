## Ingredients

- 8 oz (225 g) short pasta (shells, penne, or cavatappi)
- 1 cup (about 200 g) leftover roasted squash flesh, skin removed
- 1 tbsp butter
- 1 clove garlic, minced
- 1/2 cup hot pasta water, plus more as needed
- 1/2 cup (1 oz) grated Parmesan, plus more for serving
- 1/4 tsp kosher salt, plus more for pasta water
- Pinch of nutmeg
- Black pepper to taste
- Optional: 1 (15 oz) can white beans, drained and rinsed
- Optional: 6–8 sage leaves

## Equipment

- Large pot
- Colander
- Blender (or immersion blender in a tall cup)
- Medium skillet

## Prep-ahead notes

- Sauce (through step 3) can be made up to 3 days ahead and refrigerated. Thin with hot pasta water when tossing.
- Finished pasta keeps 3 days. Reheat with a splash of water, covered.

## Instructions

1. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil. Cook pasta to al dente per package directions. Reserve 1 cup of pasta water before draining.
2. While the pasta cooks, melt the butter in the skillet over medium heat. Add garlic (and sage leaves, if using) and cook 1 minute until fragrant. Remove the sage leaves and set aside for garnish if they crisped.
3. Blend the squash, garlic butter, 1/2 cup hot pasta water, Parmesan, salt, and nutmeg until completely smooth. The sauce should be the consistency of heavy cream; add more pasta water a tablespoon at a time if too thick.
4. Return the drained pasta to the pot over low heat. Pour in the sauce and white beans, if using. Toss 1–2 minutes until the sauce clings to the pasta, loosening with pasta water as needed.
5. Season with black pepper. Serve with extra Parmesan and the crisp sage.

## Notes

- Scaled for 1 cup of leftover squash, which feeds two adults and a toddler. Double everything for a full pound of pasta.
- Eats like mac and cheese, so it passes with the toddler without modification. Skip the black pepper on their portion.
- White beans add protein and make it a complete meal; without them, serve alongside beans or a salad.
- A teaspoon of white miso blended into the sauce adds depth if the squash was roasted plain.
- Acorn squash blends slightly stringy. Blending a full minute fixes it; a food processor does not.

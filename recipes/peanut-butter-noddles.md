## Ingredients

- 8 oz pasta (spaghetti, linguine, or udon)
- 1/4 cup peanut butter (creamy)
- 2 tbsp butter
- 2 tbsp grated parmesan
- 2 tbsp soy sauce
- 1/4–1/2 cup reserved pasta water

### Optional add-ins (recommended)

- 1 tsp fresh ginger, grated
- 1/2 tbsp maple syrup
- 1 tsp garlic, grated
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil

### Protein & veg (pick 1–2)

- 1 block firm tofu, pressed & cubed (roasted or pan-fried)
- 1 head broccoli, cut into florets (roasted or steamed)
- 2 fake chicken patties, cooked and sliced

## Equipment

- Large pot for pasta
- Skillet or sheet pan for tofu/broccoli
- Whisk or fork
- Microplane (if using fresh garlic/ginger)

## Prep-ahead notes

- Tofu can be pressed and cubed the night before; roast at 400°F for ~25 min while pasta cooks.
- Broccoli can be pre-cut and roasted alongside tofu on the same sheet pan.
- Sauce can be whisked together up to 2 days ahead; thin with hot pasta water at serving time.

## Instructions

1. Preheat oven to 400°F. Toss cubed tofu and broccoli florets with a bit of oil and salt; roast 20–25 min until browned.
2. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook pasta to al dente. **Reserve 1/2 cup pasta water** before draining.
3. In the empty warm pot (off heat), whisk peanut butter, butter, parmesan, soy sauce, and any optional add-ins with 1/4 cup hot pasta water until smooth.
4. Add drained pasta to the sauce; toss to coat, adding more pasta water as needed for a silky consistency.
5. Fold in tofu and broccoli (or top individual bowls). Serve immediately.

## Notes

- The parmesan is unusual for a peanut sauce but adds savory depth — don't skip.
- Toddler tip: serve noodles + broccoli + tofu separately on the plate before saucing; some toddlers prefer components.
- Sauce tightens as it cools; splash of hot water revives leftovers.
- **Suggested change:** Add a squeeze of lime or rice vinegar (1–2 tsp) at the end for brightness — the current recipe leans rich/salty without much acid.
- **Suggested change:** Bumping garlic to 2 tsp and ginger to 1 tsp (from 1/2) makes the flavor pop more if you like it bold.
- Fake chicken patties feel like an odd match here texturally — tofu integrates better. Consider edamame as an easy protein swap.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup (240 g) roasted squash puree, drained (see step 1; about 1 cup of leftover roasted acorn squash flesh)
- 2 large eggs
- 1/2 cup (100 g) neutral oil or melted butter
- 3/4 cup (150 g) brown sugar
- 1 3/4 cups (210 g) all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp baking soda
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1 1/2 tsp cinnamon
- 1/2 tsp ground ginger
- 1/2 tsp nutmeg
- Optional: 1/2 cup raisins, chopped dates, or pepitas for the tops

## Equipment

- 12-cup muffin tin with liners (or greased)
- Blender or food processor
- Fine-mesh sieve
- 2 mixing bowls
- Whisk

## Prep-ahead notes

- Puree can be made and drained up to 3 days ahead; keep refrigerated.
- Muffins keep 3 days at room temperature in a sealed container, or freeze up to 2 months. Reheat frozen muffins 20–30 seconds in the microwave.

## Instructions

1. Scoop the roasted squash flesh off the skin. Blend until completely smooth. Set the puree in a fine-mesh sieve over a bowl and let it drain 15–20 minutes. Discard the liquid. Measure 1 cup (240 g) after draining.
2. Preheat oven to 375°F (190°C). Line the muffin tin.
3. In the large bowl, whisk together the squash puree, eggs, oil, and brown sugar until smooth.
4. In the second bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, salt, cinnamon, ginger, and nutmeg.
5. Add the dry ingredients to the wet and fold with a spatula until just combined. A few streaks of flour are fine. Fold in raisins or dates if using.
6. Divide the batter among the 12 cups (about 3/4 full). Sprinkle pepitas on top if using.
7. Bake 18–22 minutes, until a toothpick in the center comes out clean and the tops spring back.
8. Cool in the tin 5 minutes, then move to a rack.

## Notes

- Draining is the important step. Acorn squash is much wetter than canned pumpkin, and an undrained puree gives gummy centers. If the drained puree still looks loose, hold back 2 tbsp of it.
- This puree substitutes 1:1 for canned pumpkin, so any pumpkin muffin or bread recipe works with it.
- Toddler tip: reduce brown sugar to 1/2 cup (100 g); the squash carries enough sweetness.
- Any roasted winter squash works (butternut, kabocha, delicata). Butternut and kabocha need less draining.

## Ingredients

- 4 large dried guajillo or New Mexico chiles (mild, ~1,000–2,500 SHU), stemmed and seeded
- 1–2 dried árbol chiles (hot, ~15,000–30,000 SHU), stemmed and seeded — omit for very mild
- 1 tbsp coriander seeds
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 1/2 tsp white peppercorns (or black)
- 1 stalk lemongrass, tough outer layers removed, tender core thinly sliced (about 2 tbsp)
- 1 (2-inch) piece fresh ginger, peeled and roughly chopped
- 1 (1-inch) piece fresh galangal, peeled and chopped (or an extra 1-inch ginger if unavailable)
- 6 cloves garlic
- 2 medium shallots, peeled and quartered (about 1/2 cup)
- 1 tbsp tomato paste (adds red color + umami)
- 2 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tbsp soy sauce (subs for fish sauce; keeps it vegetarian)
- 1 tsp lime zest
- 2 tbsp neutral oil, plus more as needed

## Equipment

- Small dry skillet
- Food processor
- Small heatproof bowl

## Prep-ahead notes

- Keeps 2 weeks in fridge (surface film of oil helps preservation), or 6 months frozen.
- **Best storage:** freeze in 1-tbsp portions in an ice cube tray, then transfer to a zip bag.
- 1 tbsp paste ≈ mild heat per 4 servings; 2 tbsp = medium; 3 tbsp = spicy.

## Instructions

1. Place dried chiles in a heatproof bowl, cover with boiling water, and soak 15 min until soft. Drain, reserving 2 tbsp soaking liquid.
2. Meanwhile, toast coriander, cumin, and peppercorns in a dry skillet over medium heat 2–3 min until fragrant. Grind in a spice grinder or mortar (or pulse in food processor first, before adding wet ingredients).
3. Add all ingredients to food processor: soaked chiles, ground spices, lemongrass, ginger, galangal, garlic, shallots, tomato paste, salt, soy sauce, lime zest, oil, and 2 tbsp reserved chile water.
4. Process 3–5 min, scraping sides frequently, until a thick, smooth paste forms. Add oil 1 tsp at a time if needed to help it blend.
5. Transfer to jar (fridge) or ice cube tray (freezer).

## Notes

- **Heat control:** guajillos give color + mild warmth; árbol chiles are where the heat lives. Adjust árbol count from 0 (kid-safe) to 4 (spicy).
- Galangal has a piney, citrusy note distinct from ginger; worth seeking out at an Asian market but ginger works fine.
- Fresh lemongrass freezes well — buy a bundle, slice, and freeze extras.
- Sub 1 tbsp red miso for soy sauce for deeper umami.
- Toddler-safe portioning: use 1 tsp paste per adult serving; make toddler's portion separately or reserve before adding paste.

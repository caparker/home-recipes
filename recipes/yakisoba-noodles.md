## Ingredients

- 1 recipe Sesame-Shoyu Yakisoba Sauce (see sesame-shoyu-yakisoba-sauce.md)
- 12 oz fresh yakisoba noodles (or 10 oz dry ramen/lo mein), see notes
- 14 oz extra-firm tofu, pressed and cut into 1/2-inch cubes
- 2 tbsp peanut or vegetable oil, divided
- 1/2 small green cabbage (about 3 cups), cut into 1-inch squares
- 2 medium carrots, cut into thin matchsticks
- 1 small yellow onion, thinly sliced
- 4 oz shiitake or cremini mushrooms, sliced
- 3 scallions, cut into 1-inch pieces (whites and greens separated)
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp fresh ginger, minced
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil (for finishing)
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds (for garnish)
- Pickled ginger (beni shoga) or aonori, optional, for serving

## Equipment

- Wok or large (12-inch) skillet
- Chef's knife
- Tongs
- Small bowl
- Paper towels (for pressing tofu)

## Prep-ahead notes

- Sauce: make up to 2 weeks ahead; store in fridge.
- Tofu: press and cube up to 1 day ahead; store covered in fridge.
- Vegetables: slice up to 1 day ahead; store in airtight container with a paper towel to absorb moisture.
- Noodles: if using fresh/pre-cooked yakisoba noodles, loosen under warm water right before cooking. Don't pre-cook dry noodles more than 30 minutes ahead or they'll clump.

## Instructions

1. Press tofu between paper towels under a heavy pan for 15 minutes. Cut into 1/2-inch cubes and pat dry again.
2. If using dry noodles, cook per package until just shy of al dente, drain, rinse with cold water, and toss with 1 tsp oil to prevent sticking. If using fresh yakisoba noodles, rinse under warm water and gently separate.
3. Heat 1 tbsp oil in wok over medium-high until shimmering. Add tofu in a single layer; cook undisturbed 2-3 minutes per side until golden on at least 2 sides. Transfer to a plate.
4. Add remaining 1 tbsp oil to the wok. Add onion, carrots, and scallion whites; stir-fry 2 minutes until onion softens slightly.
5. Add mushrooms and 1/2 tsp salt; stir-fry 2 minutes until mushrooms release liquid and start to brown.
6. Add cabbage; stir-fry 2-3 minutes until just wilted but still crunchy.
7. Clear a space in the center of the wok. Add garlic and ginger; cook 30 seconds until fragrant, then toss with vegetables.
8. Return tofu to the wok. Add noodles and sauce. Toss with tongs to coat evenly, 1-2 minutes, letting the sauce sizzle against the hot pan.
9. Off heat, drizzle with finishing sesame oil and toss. Top with scallion greens and sesame seeds.
10. Serve immediately, with pickled ginger or aonori if desired.

## Notes

- Noodle options, in order of authenticity: fresh yakisoba (refrigerated section, Asian market) > fresh ramen > fresh lo mein > dry ramen (discard seasoning packet) > dry spaghetti (not ideal but works in a pinch).
- Vegetable swaps are flexible — bean sprouts, bell pepper, snow peas, bok choy, and napa cabbage all work. Aim for ~5-6 cups raw vegetables total.
- Keep the heat high and don't overcrowd — this is a stir-fry, not a braise. If your burner is weak, cook vegetables in 2 batches.
- Toddler tip: pull a portion of plain cooked noodles + a few pieces of cabbage/carrot/tofu aside before adding sauce. The adult sauce is salty for under-2s.
- Omnivore adaptation: swap tofu for 12 oz thinly sliced pork belly or chicken thigh; cook first, remove, then proceed with vegetables.
- The sesame-forward profile really comes through when the sauce hits the hot wok at the end — don't skip step 8's sizzle.
